Draft REACH Annex XVII Restriction on Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS) -- Background and Status

Clause 1: Definition and scope
The restriction dossier defines PFAS structurally: any substance that contains at least one fully fluorinated methyl (CF3-) or methylene (-CF2-) carbon atom (without any H/Cl/Br/I attached to it), with limited exceptions. This structural definition is why no single enumerated CAS list can represent "all PFAS in scope" -- ECHA's impact assessment estimate of roughly 14,000 substances falling under this definition is a modelled estimate, not a published registry. Named PFAS with assigned CAS numbers (as used for screening in this system) are a verifiable, citable subset of that structural universe, not its entirety.

Clause 2: Regulatory history and status
The restriction proposal was submitted on 13 January 2023 by the national authorities of Germany, the Netherlands, Denmark, Sweden and Norway together with ECHA. The first public consultation ran until 25 September 2023 and drew over 5,600 comments. An updated PFAS Background Document, narrowing the scope of some sectors and revising the proposed concentration thresholds, was published on 20 August 2025. ECHA's Risk Assessment Committee (RAC) adopted its opinion on 3 March 2026; the Socio-Economic Analysis Committee (SEAC) agreed a draft opinion on 11 March 2026 and opened a second public consultation on that draft opinion on 26 March 2026, running until 25 May 2026. SEAC's final opinion is expected by the end of 2026. As of this system's build date (5 July 2026), the restriction is still in the committee-opinion/consultation stage: the European Commission has not yet drafted a formal restriction decision, and a final Annex XVII entry is not expected before 2027 at the earliest. Screening against these draft thresholds is therefore a forward-looking readiness check, not enforcement of a substance restriction that is currently in force.

Clause 3: Proposed concentration thresholds
The updated proposal sets out three enforceable concentration limits for PFAS in a substance, mixture, or article placed on the EU market:
(a) 25 ppb (parts per billion) for any individual PFAS substance, measured by targeted analysis; polymeric PFAS are excluded from this quantification.
(b) 250 ppb for the sum of all quantifiable PFAS (total PFAS), by targeted analysis; pre-degradation of PFAS precursors to enable quantification is optional under this limit; polymeric PFAS are excluded.
(c) 50 ppm (parts per million) for total PFAS content including polymeric PFAS, intended as a broader screening trigger: where total fluorine content exceeds 50 mg F/kg, the manufacturer, importer, or downstream user must be able to demonstrate to enforcement authorities that the fluorine present does not originate from PFAS.
None of these three thresholds is "the" PFAS limit -- a substance can be compliant against the 25 ppb individual-substance limit while the product as a whole still fails the 250 ppb total-PFAS or 50 ppm polymeric-inclusive limits, so all three must be checked.

Clause 4: Derogation pathways
The updated proposal offers two restriction options under consideration: RO1, a full ban with an 18-month transition period after entry into force, and RO2, time-limited use-specific exemptions ranging from 5 to 12 years depending on the sector's demonstrated viability of alternatives. Which pathway applies (and to which specific use) will be decided sector-by-sector in the final restriction text; until that text exists, this system cannot determine whether a given use qualifies for a derogation, and treats PFAS-substance detections above the draft thresholds as NEEDS_REVIEW screening flags rather than definitive non-compliance findings, consistent with the "screening result for review, not a legal determination" framing required throughout this system.

Clause 5: Sector coverage
The August 2025 updated Background Document narrowed the originally near-universal sector coverage but retained coverage of, among others: textiles and apparel (including durable water repellents), metal plating and surface coatings, semiconductors, medical devices, food-contact materials, firefighting foam, and construction products. A supplier document describing a surface coating, textile treatment, or metal-finishing process should be treated as within the PFAS restriction's likely scope for screening purposes, even though the final sector list has not been legally adopted.
